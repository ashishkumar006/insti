THROUGH THE LENS: CULTURAL AFFAIRS -
ARTS & LITERARY
The Cultural Sphere at IIT Madras brings creativity to life through year-round artistic engagement. Saarang,
India’s largest student-run fest, spans five days, hosts 100+ events, and draws a footfall of 80,000. Sangam
unites 18 clubs across diverse art forms, engaging the student body and nurturing talent. These clubs train and
collaborate throughout the year, channeling their collective effort toward Inter IIT Culturals, where they represent
IITM with excellence, teamwork, and competitive spirit. The National Cultural Academy (NCA) enables
beginners to learn and master art forms from scratch. Through its collaboration with SPIC MACAY, the sphere
also promotes Indian classical music and heritage on campus.

THROUGH THE LENS: SPICMACAY
This year, the SPIC MACAY chapter at Indian Institute of Technology Madras hosted a vibrant series of
events across both the semesters, beginning with Samarambh 2025 and culminating in Virasat 2026.
Samarambh served as a three-day prelude celebrating Indian classical arts through workshops and
performances, including Hindustani music sessions by Suranjana Bose, Kuchipudi workshops by Jaikishore
Mosalikanti, fine arts sessions by V. K. Munusamy, and a Kathakali performance by Kalamandalam
Adithyan.
The celebration continued with the five-day Virasat 2026, featuring diverse classical performances such as
Santoor by Varsha Agarwal, Bharatanatyam by Urmila Sathyanarayana, Carnatic vocal by Neyveli
Santhanagopal, a heritage talk and live music by Madusudhan Kalaichelvan, and a Carnatic veena concert
by Jaishri & Jayaraj, along with yoga sessions and a classic film screening. Together, these festivals brought
Indian classical traditions closer to the campus community.

cultural energy.

Unified Mess System (July-Nov 2025)
The semester began with the introduction of a completely unified mess in all the messes. The aim was to reduce
strict regional separation and students should have a more balanced menu for everyone. Student feedback
throughout the semester played an important role in understanding its practicality and acceptance.
Inspection Training with HydroLabs
After the MMCC team was formed, a training session with HydroLabs helped structure the inspection process.
The focus shifted from routine checking to organised hygiene evaluation, record maintenance(well-documented),
and proper procedures.
FSSAI Training for Mess Staff
An FSSAI session was conducted for caterers, chefs, and mess staff, covering handling of ingredients, storage
practices, and careful sourcing of raw materials.
Happy Meal Initiative
Special dinners called Happy Meal were organised after Quiz-I and Quiz-II to provide a break from the regular
menu. The menus were planned by MMCC and coordinated with caterers across hostels.
Protein Mess (Jan-May 2026)
A protein-rich mess menu was introduced for students requiring higher protein intake, including sports and
fitness-focused students. The menu was designed to be workable for both vegetarians and non-vegetarians.
CMGFS All-Vendor Meet:
To streamline the functioning of all facilities, CMGFS organised the All-Vendor Meet, bringing together every
vendor across the campus which comes under our zone, along with HAS and CMGFS Chairperson. This meeting
became a breakthrough moment when issues that had persisted for months were openly discussed, vendors
shared their challenges, and solutions were collectively shaped.

The Fifth Estate (T5E) is the official news and media body of IIT Madras and serves as the primary source of
information for students about events unfolding across the institute. By providing a voice for the student
community, we act as an intermediary between the large student body and other stakeholders, including the
administration, faculty, staff, and other residents. Committed to unbiased and error-free journalism, T5E plays an
important role in maintaining an environment conducive to open discussion on a variety of issues.
T5E regularly produces content in a variety of formats, including videos, photo essays, and comic strips. Even
the written content can vary from on-ground reports to opinion pieces, editorials, interviews, and other long-form
formats. We also publish Placement and Exit Surveys, two long-standing initiatives that seek to paint a picture
of how the graduating batch every year looks back and evaluates its experiences on campus, including
placements, academics, extracurriculars, career choices, life on campus, etc. Moreover, T5E also plays a key
role in reporting on election campaigns and the actions taken by elected student representatives and the Student
Legislative Council, thus critically examining student governance within the institute.
Our team consists of three verticals: the Press vertical, which involves Press Correspondents and Editors who
conduct extensive research, including on-ground reportage and interviews, to churn out brilliant pieces, the
Media and Design vertical, which involves a vertical Head supervising Media Correspondents who form the
bulwarks of the behind-the-scenes design, photography and videography efforts, and the Research Vertical,
which consists of a Head leading a team of Analysts who work on curating, and analysing the responses to, our
campus-wide surveys. The entire team works under the oversight of two Executive Editors.

A Historic First: Research Scholar Immersion Program 2025
The Research Affairs Secretary Council (2025–26) proudly scripted history at IIT Madras by conceptualizing and
executing the inaugural Research Scholar Immersion Program (RSIP), dedicated entirely to newly joined
research scholars. Spanning two enriching weeks from July 3 to 18, 2025, the program brought together over 30
expert sessions covering academic orientation, research ethics, technical writing, AI tools, innovation, wellness,
and beyond.
With nine rotating batches seamlessly navigating lectures, lab visits to CFI, SAIF, and the Central Workshop,
alongside dynamic Outbound Training (OBT) sessions, RSIP set an entirely new standard for scholar onboarding
at IITM. Beyond the academics, RSIP fostered a sense of belonging and community that no induction had
previously offered research scholars at this scale. From the DoST ROPE sessions and Senior-Junior interactions
to the Mandela Art Workshop, Talent Show, Mr. & Miss Freshers, and a memorable DJ Night, the program wove
together intellectual growth with cultural vibrancy.

M.Tech Legislator Rulebook
The M.Tech Legislators' Rulebook was introduced to establish a clear framework for the roles, responsibilities,
and functioning of M.Tech Legislators, ensuring structured and effective representation of M.Tech students and

Separate Page for SLC in DoSt website

Hostel Council Position Optimisation Bill
The Hostel Council Position Optimisation (Amendment) Bill, 2026 restructures the Hostel Council by
consolidating overlapping Positions of Responsibility and streamlining duties to improve efficiency and
accountability in hostel governance. It aims to ensure meaningful responsibilities for each role while enhancing
student participation and administrative effectiveness.
SECC-SEC Bifurcation Bill
The SEC–SECC Bifurcation and Amendment Bill, 2026 proposes the separation of the Student Election

About SECC and SEC
The Student Ethics and Constitution Commission (SECC) and the Student Election Commission (SEC) together
form key pillars of student governance structures at IIT Madras. SECC upholds the Students’ Constitution by
ensuring that governance, accountability, and student conduct are guided by clear and fair processes, while also
serving as a structured mechanism for addressing issues within its purview. In doing so, it plays an important role
in maintaining procedural integrity and ensuring that constitutional provisions are effectively implemented across
student-led bodies.
SEC, constituted under the same framework, is responsible for conducting student elections. It ensures the
electoral process is carried out in a fair, transparent, and accessible manner, overseeing its execution in
accordance with established guidelines and reinforcing trust in student representation.

GATEKEEPER TRAINING:
Gatekeeper training was conducted on 22–24 December 2025 to strengthen the hostel-based support system for
student well-being IITM. Over three days, psychologists worked closely with wardens and hostel managers,
attenders to build both awareness and practical approaches they can use in real situations.
Training includes the symptom identification, including early signs of anxiety, depression, burnout, suicidal risk,
substance use, and academic distress, with examples tailored to different programmes and course loads.
The sessions psycho educated participants about common stressors linked to exams, placements, project
deadlines, homesickness, interpersonal conflicts, and adjustment to campus life, helping staff understand “why”
students behave the way they do.
Wardens, Managers and attenders of hostel IIT Madras were trained to observe key red flags such as withdrawal,
changes in routine, visible distress, academic disengagement, and conflict patterns, and to document and
communicate concerns appropriately.
Role-plays and scripts were used to demonstrate how to initiate conversations with students in a
non-threatening, empathetic, and confidential manner as to what to say, what to avoid, and how to listen without
minimizing or overreacting. The training also clarified when and how to escalate concerns, outlining clear referral
pathways to counsellors, psychologists, or medical support, and how to follow up without being intrusive.
A significant component focused on the emotional load carried by wardens and managers themselves, promoting
self-care strategies, boundary setting, peer support among staff, and recognizing signs of their own stress and

SAATHI is one of the largest student-run wellness initiatives at IIT Madras, functioning under the purview of the
Students’ General Secretary and the Wellness Office. The team focuses on promoting student well-being and
fostering a balanced campus environment through various initiatives and engagement activities.
SAATHI organizes workshops, awareness sessions, community events, and wellness initiatives that encourage
healthy living, peer interaction, and holistic development. The team also operates the Student Mentorship Cell,
which connects junior students with senior mentors to provide guidance on academic, personal, and
campus-related challenges.
Additionally, the Women’s Representative vertical works to strengthen women’s participation and ensure safe
spaces for dialogue and support. Through these efforts, SAATHI seeks to contribute to a campus culture that
values well-being alongside academic excellence.

Sundays on Cycle:
On June 1, 2025, SAATHI organized a special edition of Sundays on Cycle to mark the upcoming World Bicycle
Day. The initiative aligned with the spirit of the Fit India Movement and encouraged students to adopt cycling as a
healthy and environmentally sustainable practice. The event promoted physical fitness while highlighting the
importance of sustainable mobility within the campus community.
International Day Against Drug Abuse and Illicit Trafficking:
On June 26, 2025, SAATHI conducted a campus-wide awareness initiative addressing the risks associated with
substance abuse. The program began with a pledge-taking ceremony and cycling rally, followed by an address by
the Dean (Students) and an interactive workshop facilitated by the Myndwell team, the initiative concluded with a
film screening. We engaged over 200 students and promoted awareness about the cause.
Session on Wellbeing:
Introductory sessions aimed at mental, physical and overall wellbeing were conducted and students were told
about how to overcome common challenges faced by the students in their first year in the campus. We tried to
positively shape the journey of the students through these sessions so that they can tackle the upcoming
challenges smoothly.
Awareness on Nutrition & Health:
The session focused on how to raise awareness about balanced dietary habits and their impact on overall
well-being. We witnessed enthusiastic participation from students, with many actively engaging in discussions and
raising questions during and after the talk. The speaker highlighted practical approaches to maintaining healthier
eating patterns within the demanding routine of campus life. The session encouraged students to reflect on their
own lifestyle choices and adopt sustainable habits that support long-term physical and mental well-being.
Breaking the Barriers:
In collaboration with the MITR team, SAATHI organized Breaking the Barriers, a fun-filling event aimed at
encouraging students to participate openly. This included making poses, pushing their limits physically and
mentally through games. The event fostered a welcoming environment where students could share perspectives,
learn from one another, feel more confident and comfortable.
Drug Free India Campaign:
In the light of ‘Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan’, team Saathi, in association with Myndwell, conducted drug
awareness and mental health sessions in the hostels on August 16, 2026. The session played a vital role in
equipping students with accurate information about drugs and promoting informed choices among students,
fostering a prevention-oriented mindset.

to step outside their usual circles, unwind and build meaningful connections within the campus community.

Vision
Inclusive Education of Students with Special Needs, IIT Madras, seeks to nurture students with disabilities (SwD)
so that they become personally independent and professionally competent, by enabling participation in curricular,
co-curricular and extra-curricular activities in the IIT campus. IITM follows the mandates of both national and
international bodies while planning and implementing services for the students with special needs.
Mission
To integrate students with disabilities into mainstream education, bringing all learners together regardless of
strengths or limitations.
Message from the Advisor
At IIT Madras, one of India's premier institutions of higher learning, we
are committed to building a campus that is not only excellent but
equitable. As the Advisor for Inclusive Education to the Dean of
Students, my mandate is to ensure that every student at IIT Madras,
regardless of disability, background, or circumstance, has full and
meaningful access to the academic, social, and cultural life of this
institution and a state of the art ecosystem. IIT Madras is home to
students of exceptional talent and determination, many of whom
navigate their educational journey with physical or sensory impairments
that require not just accommodation, but genuine institutional
commitment. Through the Office of Inclusive Education, we work closely with students, faculty, departments and
administrative units across the campus to facilitate academic accommodation, improve accessibility
infrastructure, foster awareness, and advocate for policy that places inclusion at the center of institutional
decision-making.

1 Project Mozhi – Pilot Phase II
An English communication and confidence-building program was conducted at Women’s ITI, Guindy, in
collaboration with the Department of Employment and Training, Government of Tamil Nadu. Over five structured
sessions, students engaged in spoken English activities, digital literacy tools, role-plays, skits, and a final
showcase. The program focused on bridging the gap between comprehension and expression while nurturing
confidence and self-belief.
Key highlights:
• Curriculum redesigned with digital tools (Google Translate, Duolingo, ChatGPT)
• Emphasis on sentence formation, public speaking, and confidence
• Final showcase with skits and business pitches in English
2 Village Immersion Visit – Edaikazhinadu, Tamil Nadu
A full-day immersive village visit focusing on healthcare, education, agriculture, water systems, culture, and rural
livelihoods. Students interacted closely with villagers, schools, farmers, and local institutions to understand
traditional knowledge systems alongside modern challenges.
Focus areas:
• Education and mental well-being in rural schools
• Indigenous farming, soil health, and sustainability
• Healthcare access, sanitation, and WASH infrastructure
• Arts, culture, temples, and community life
• Rural economy, salt production, and livelihood diversification
3 Food Stalls – Rural Women–Led Entrepreneurship
During Shaastra and Saarang, IViL facilitated food stalls run by rural women entrepreneurs, who cooked and
served authentic South Indian cuisine on campus.
This initiative was designed to create real, dignified income opportunities while encouraging an entrepreneurial
mindset among rural women. IViL’s role was limited to logistics, coordination, permissions, and operational
support, ensuring that the profits directly went to the women themselves.
The stalls became vibrant spaces of cultural exchange where food told stories of home, tradition, and
self-reliance. For students, it was a lesson in enabling livelihoods rather than leading them; for the women, it was
a platform to showcase skill, confidence, and independence in a high-visibility setting.

The National Service Scheme, IIT Madras is a student-run organization established over four decades ago with a
vision to contribute meaningfully to NSS society in every possible way.
At NSS IIT Madras, our mission is to mobilize and inspire youth to become progressive, socially responsible
citizens. We strive to instill in them a deep sense of empathy, leadership, and commitment toward community
service, empowering them to drive positive and sustainable change.
The National Service Scheme, IIT Madras operates through six dedicated verticals: Events, IViL (IIT for Villages),
Products, Projects, PR/Web Operations, and Avanti. Each vertical plays a distinct and complementary role,
ensuring that the organization’s initiatives span social awareness, rural development, innovation, educational
outreach, communication, and strategic implementation.
Total Volunteers: 1600+
The six verticals of the National Service Scheme, IIT Madras are led by over 25 dedicated team heads and
coordinators. Each vertical NSS Advisor Prof. Rupen Goswami comprises a strong and active group of 100+ NSS
volunteers who collectively plan, organize, and execute events with efficiency and commitment.
The vertical teams are responsible for successfully coordinating initiatives from the planning stage to execution
and post-event documentation. Since NSS is a compulsory credit-based course at IIT Madras, event credits are
systematically allotted to volunteers by the respective coordinators based on participation and contribution.
Additionally, NSS head and vertical team heads conduct regular group meetings to finalize event plans and credit
structures. These decisions are made in the presence of, and with approval from, the NSS Faculty Advisor to
ensure transparency, accountability, and proper academic alignment

The NSS Head of the National Service Scheme, IIT Madras is responsible for
the overall management and coordination of student-led activities across all
verticals. The role involves providing strategic direction, ensuring smooth

objectives.

coordinating industrial visits, and facilitating the patent filing process for innovation-driven projects. The NSS Head

collaboration among them.
In addition, the NSS Head mobilizes volunteers for institute-level programs at IIT Madras and represents the
organization in ministry-level and external events, ensuring strong institutional engagement and outreach.
